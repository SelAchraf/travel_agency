ALGERIAN TRAVEL AGENCY
Website & Management Platform
Functional Requirements & Product Scope

A practical specification for a website you can build, demonstrate, and sell to a travel agency in
Algeria.

Product type

Public travel website + private agency back-office + optional customer portal

Primary market

Small and medium-sized travel agencies in Algeria

Languages

Arabic (RTL), French (LTR), with English as an optional later addition

Currency

Algerian dinar (DZD / DA)

Delivery approach

Start with a manageable MVP; add integrations and advanced workflows later

Prepared as a product-planning document • Version 1.0 • November 2026

1. Product vision
The product should help a travel agency present its services online and manage the daily work behind those services
from one system.
Recommended product: a responsive public website connected to a secure administration dashboard. Customers
discover trips and submit booking requests; agency employees manage customers, offers, reservations, documents and
payments.
Important scope decision: the first version should manage booking requests and agency-confirmed reservations. It
does not need to issue airline tickets, connect to live airline inventory, or guarantee live hotel availability. Those
integrations are separate, more complex projects.
The platform should be configurable for each agency: its name, logo, contact details, branches, services,
branding, languages and published offers.

1.1 Main goals
• Give the agency a professional online presence that works well on phones.
• Turn website visitors into enquiries and booking requests.
• Keep customer, reservation, payment and document information organized.
• Reduce duplicate data entry, paper tracking and spreadsheet dependence.
• Give the owner a clear overview of bookings, outstanding balances and upcoming departures.

1.2 User groups
User

Main needs

Customer

booking progress.

2. Public website functionalities
This is the part visible to everyone. It should be fast, trustworthy, mobile-friendly and easy for agency staff to update.

2.1 Essential pages

Required functionality

Home

Agency introduction, featured offers, popular destinations, services, trust indicators and clear
enquiry/WhatsApp calls to action.

About the agency

Agency profile, experience, values, licence/accreditation details where applicable, address

Travel offers

Searchable/filterable list of published trips and packages, with price, dates, destination and

Offer details

Description, itinerary, departure/return dates, price, included/excluded services,

Services

Separate pages for tourism, domestic trips, international trips, hotel reservations, visa

Destinations

Contact

2.2 Travel offer catalogue
• Staff can create, edit, publish, unpublish and archive offers.
• Each offer can have destination, trip type, start/end dates, duration, price in DZD, pricing basis (per person, room, family,
etc.), capacity, hotel/accommodation, transport details and description.
• Support photos, itinerary/day-by-day programme, included services, excluded services and downloadable brochure.
• Display clear offer states such as Available, Limited places, Request availability and Fully booked. Do not show live
availability unless the agency actually maintains it.
• Allow filters such as destination, trip type, date range and budget where the agency has enough offers to justify them.

2.3 Enquiry and booking request forms
• Collect the customer's name, phone number, email (optional or required by agency policy), selected offer, number of
travellers, preferred dates and message.
• Validate required fields and show a clear confirmation after submission.
• Create an enquiry record in the dashboard and notify the responsible agency staff.
• Record consent to the privacy notice where required.
• Clearly explain that submitting a request is not a confirmed reservation until the agency confirms it.

2.4 Trust, usability and conversion
• Prominent phone and WhatsApp contact actions on mobile.
• Real agency address, map, office hours and verified contact details.
• Testimonials only when genuine and approved for publication.
• Clear prices, conditions, inclusions and exclusions; avoid hidden fees.
• Accessible typography, readable contrast, optimized images and simple navigation.

3. Agency administration dashboard
The back-office is the core operational product. Only authenticated and authorized staff should access it.

3.1 Dashboard overview
• Show enquiries awaiting response, active reservations, upcoming departures and tasks requiring attention.
• Show total payments received and outstanding customer balances for a selected period.
• Provide shortcuts to add a customer, create an offer, register a reservation and record a payment.
• Allow filtering by date, employee, trip and reservation status.

3.2 Customer / CRM management
• Create, view, edit, archive and search customer profiles.
• Store name, contact details, preferred language and internal notes.
• Store traveller information only when needed for a booking, with restricted access to sensitive fields.
• View a customer's enquiries, reservations, payments and relevant documents in one history.
• Detect likely duplicate customers by phone number or email and let staff review matches.

3.3 Enquiry management
• Receive requests submitted through the website and allow staff to create enquiries manually (phone, walk-in or
WhatsApp).
• Assign an enquiry to an employee and set a follow-up date.
• Track status: New, Contacted, Waiting for customer, Converted, Rejected or Closed.
• Keep a timestamped activity history and internal notes.

3.4 Reservation management
• Create a reservation from an enquiry or directly from the dashboard.
• Link the reservation to a customer, offer, travellers and responsible employee.
• Store booking date, travel dates, passenger count, agreed price, discount if authorized and special requirements.
• Track status: Pending, Awaiting documents, Awaiting payment, Confirmed, Cancelled or Completed.
• Generate a reservation reference number and printable confirmation.
• Maintain a history of status changes; do not silently overwrite important changes.

3.5 Group trips and capacity
• Create a departure/group linked to an offer, with departure date, return date and maximum capacity.
• Track booked places, available places and a waiting list if needed.
• Prevent staff from confirming more places than the configured capacity unless a manager explicitly overrides it.
• Allow one offer to have multiple departure dates.

3.6 Payment tracking
• Record an agreed total price, deposits, subsequent instalments and remaining balance.
• Record payment date, amount, method, reference and employee who entered it.
• Support cash, bank transfer and other payment methods configured by the agency.
• Generate a receipt for each recorded payment and show the payment history.
• Flag overdue or incomplete balances based on agency-defined due dates.
• Keep an audit trail for edits, cancellations and refunds.

4. Documents, employees and reports
4.1 Document management
• Attach files to a customer or reservation, with document type, upload date, expiry date (if relevant) and status.
• Examples: passport copy, identity document, visa-related file, contract, itinerary, insurance document and payment receipt.
• Use private storage; never expose uploaded identity or passport documents through public URLs.
• Restrict document access by role and log sensitive access or changes where practical.
• Support download, replacement and deletion according to the agency's retention policy and applicable law.

4.2 Employee and access management
Role

Suggested permissions

Owner / Administrator

All modules, agency settings, users, reports and permission management.

Manager

Operational modules, staff assignments, reports and approved overrides.

Reservation agent

Customers, enquiries, offers (if permitted), reservations and trip documents.

Finance employee

Payment records, receipts, balances and financial reports; limited access to unrelated
documents.

Content editor

Public website pages, destinations, images and offers; no access to private
financial/customer records unless explicitly granted.

Use role-based access control (RBAC), individual staff accounts, secure password handling, session management and
account deactivation when an employee leaves.

4.3 Reports and exports
• Reservations by period, destination, offer and status.
• Revenue collected versus amounts still due; clearly distinguish cash received from booked sales.
• Upcoming departures, passenger lists and capacity summaries.
• Enquiry conversion: received, followed up, converted and closed.
• Export permitted data to CSV or PDF for operational use.
• Apply date filters and restrict financial reports to authorized roles.

4.4 Notifications and communication
• In-dashboard alerts for new enquiries, upcoming departures, missing documents and due payments.
• Email notifications for enquiry receipt and reservation updates if email is configured.
• WhatsApp click-to-chat links with prefilled message templates; sending automated WhatsApp messages requires an
approved provider/API and setup.
• Allow staff to record that a customer was contacted and when.

5. Optional customer portal
The portal can be delivered after the public website and agency dashboard are working. It adds value but should not
delay the first usable release.
• Customer receives a secure invitation or booking link associated with their reservation.
• Customer can view reservation reference, trip details, current status and payment balance.
• Customer can upload requested documents through a protected upload flow.
• Customer can download confirmation, itinerary and receipts.
• Customer can submit a question or request a correction.
• The portal must show only that customer's information and must not expose other travellers' records.

6. Algeria-specific requirements
Area

Requirement / design consideration

Languages

Arabic and French at minimum; Arabic pages use proper right-to-left layout, French pages use
left-to-right layout. Keep translations manageable in the admin system.

Currency

Display DZD / DA consistently. Store amounts as decimal values and keep currency explicit in
financial records.

Contact details

Support Algerian mobile/landline formats and configurable agency contact numbers.

Dates and time

Use clear date formats and Algeria local time for operational schedules; store timestamps
consistently in the backend.

Payments

Start with manual recording of cash and bank transfers. Add local electronic payment
integrations only after checking provider eligibility, commercial terms and technical
documentation.

Documents

Treat passport and identity documents as highly sensitive. Minimize collection, limit access,
protect storage and define retention/deletion rules.

Legal and tax

Have the agency and its accountant/legal adviser validate contract wording, invoice/receipt
formats, tax handling, privacy notices and sector-specific requirements.

Hosting

Choose hosting based on data protection, reliability, support, backups, latency and the agency's
contractual needs.

Connectivity

Optimize for mobile networks and slower connections: compressed images, light pages and
graceful form recovery.

Do not claim that the software is legally or tax compliant merely because it generates an invoice or stores
customer data. Confirm the current Algerian requirements with qualified local professionals.

8. Core business workflows
8.1 Website enquiry to confirmed reservation
Step

System behaviour

1. Discover

Visitor opens an offer and reads its dates, price and conditions.

2. Request

Visitor submits a booking/enquiry form.

3. Receive

System creates an enquiry and alerts the agency team.

4. Follow up

Employee contacts the customer, records notes and updates enquiry status.

5. Confirm details

Employee checks availability and requirements with the agency's actual suppliers/processes.

6. Reserve

Employee creates or confirms the reservation and assigns a reference.

7. Payment

Employee records deposit or instalment and generates a receipt.

8. Prepare

Employee tracks required documents and upcoming departure tasks.

9. Complete

Reservation is marked completed, cancelled or otherwise closed with a recorded reason.

8.2 Key business rules
• An enquiry is not automatically a confirmed booking.
• A reservation should not be marked confirmed until an authorized employee has checked the agency's required conditions.
• The remaining balance is calculated from the agreed total minus recorded payments, with refunds and adjustments
represented explicitly.
• Every reservation must have a unique reference.
• Changing an offer price must not silently change the agreed price of an existing reservation.
• Deleting financial or reservation history should be restricted; prefer cancellation, archive or reversal records.
• A departure's capacity must be checked before confirming a new place.

9. Non-functional requirements: quality, security and
reliability
Category

Requirement

Security

HTTPS in production; password hashing; input validation; protection against common web
vulnerabilities; secure session/token handling.

Authorization

Enforce permissions on the backend for every protected operation, not only by hiding frontend
buttons.

Sensitive files

Private file storage, access checks, upload size/type limits, malware scanning where available,
and no public document links.

Privacy

Collect only necessary personal data; publish a clear privacy notice; define retention and deletion
procedures.

Auditability

Record important actions such as payment edits, reservation status changes, permission
changes and document updates.

Backups

Automated database backups, protected backup access and periodically tested restore
procedures.

Reliability

Handle failed form submissions and notification errors safely; avoid duplicate reservations on
repeated requests.

Performance

Optimize images, paginate long lists, index commonly searched database fields and avoid
loading unnecessary data.

Accessibility

Keyboard-friendly forms, readable contrast, labels and clear validation messages.

Maintainability

Use modular code, documented APIs, migrations, automated tests and separate
development/production settings.

Localization

Support RTL/LTR layouts, translation files, localized dates and consistent currency formatting.

Minimum test scenarios
• A visitor can submit a valid request; invalid requests show useful validation messages.
• An unauthorized user cannot open the admin dashboard or call protected API endpoints.
• An employee cannot view a document or financial record outside their permission scope.
• A customer cannot access another customer's reservation by changing a URL or identifier.
• Repeated form submission does not unintentionally create duplicate reservations.
• Payment totals and outstanding balances remain correct after multiple instalments and refunds.
• A database backup can be restored in a test environment.

10. Suggested technical structure
A modular monolith is recommended for the first version: one backend application organized into clear modules, one
relational database and one frontend. This is easier to build and deploy than microservices while leaving room to scale
later.

Module

Responsibilities

auth

Login, password reset, sessions/tokens, roles and permissions.

agency

Agency profile, branding, branches, contact details and settings.

website_content

Pages, services, destinations, FAQs, images and published content.

offers

Travel packages, departure dates, pricing and publication status.

customers

Customer profiles, traveller details and customer history.

enquiries

Website requests, follow-ups, assignment and status history.

reservations

Bookings, passengers, status, reference and trip association.

payments

Payments, receipts, balances, refunds and financial audit trail.

documents

Protected file metadata, access rules and document status.

notifications

Email/in-app notification records and delivery status.

reports

Aggregated operational and financial summaries.

10.2 Suggested core database entities
Agency, User, Role, Permission, Branch, WebsitePage, Service, Destination, TravelOffer, Departure, Customer,
Traveller, Enquiry, EnquiryActivity, Reservation, ReservationTraveller, Payment, Refund, Document, Notification,
AuditLog.
Use foreign keys and database constraints for relationships and important business rules. Keep a reservation's agreed
price as a snapshot so later offer edits do not rewrite historical bookings.

11. Features to postpone (avoid making the first version
too complex)
• Live flight search, airline ticket issuance and real-time seat inventory.
• Live hotel inventory and automatic supplier booking.
• Automatic visa approval predictions or promises.
• Complex accounting, payroll and tax filing.
• Native iOS/Android apps; make the website responsive first.
• AI chatbot, recommendation engine and automatic pricing.
• Microservices, Kubernetes and complicated distributed infrastructure.
• Multi-agency marketplace where many agencies sell through one public platform.

These features may be valuable later, but they depend on external providers, legal/commercial agreements or
substantial operational complexity.

12. Preparing the product to sell to an agency
A software product sold to an agency needs more than features. It must be configurable, supportable and easy to hand
over.
• Agency branding: logo, colors, contact details, office locations and social links.
• Content control: agency staff can update offers, photos, pages and FAQs without editing code.
• Deployment and domain setup: production domain, HTTPS, backups and environment configuration.
• Training: short user guide and onboarding session for the agency's employees.
• Support: a defined process for bug reports, updates, backups and recovery.
• Data ownership: clear agreement about who owns customer data, how it can be exported and what happens if the contract
ends.
• Commercial terms: setup fee, hosting/maintenance fee, support scope, update policy and any third-party costs.
• Acceptance testing: run realistic workflows with agency staff before final delivery.

12.1 Suggested acceptance checklist
• Agency can log in and manage staff accounts.
• Agency can publish and unpublish a travel offer.
• A visitor can submit an enquiry from a phone.
• Staff can convert the enquiry into a reservation.
• Staff can record multiple payments and produce a receipt.
• Staff can attach a private document and retrieve it only with permission.
• Arabic and French layouts display correctly.
• Owner can view basic booking and payment reports.
• Backups and restore procedure are demonstrated.
• Agency staff have tested the system and approved the workflow.

14. Assumptions and items to confirm with the agency
• Which services does the agency actually sell: outbound trips, domestic tourism, Umrah/Hajj, visa assistance, hotels,
transport or other services?
• Does the agency need only enquiry forms, or does it already have supplier systems/APIs for confirmed bookings?
• How does it currently track customers, reservations, deposits, instalments, refunds and expenses?
• Which documents does it collect, who can access them and how long must they be retained?
• Which payment methods does it accept, and what receipt/invoice format does its accountant require?
• Does it have multiple branches, employees and approval steps?
• Who will update the website offers and content after launch?
• Does it require Arabic and French from launch, and who will provide/approve translations?
• What hosting, support, backup and data-export arrangements does the agency expect?

Final product principle: build around the agency's real workflow, not a long feature checklist. A smaller system that staff
use every day is more valuable than a large system that is difficult to operate.
